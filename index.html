Barstow Community College Classes

Scroll down to explore the core classes I teach in the Computer Business Information Systems (CBIS) program.

CBIS 1: Computer Concepts

An introduction to computer concepts, covering hardware, software, operating systems, and the basics of networking and security. This course prepares students for entry-level IT roles and sets the foundation for Microsoft Specialist certifications.

CBIS 13: Management Information Systems

Focuses on how businesses use technology to solve problems and manage data. Covers systems analysis, hardware/software methodologies, and the organizational impact of technology deployments.

CBIS 14: Internet Security

Delves into configuring, diagnosing, and supporting hardware/software against security threats. Explores methodologies hackers use and the hands-on techniques required to defend local area networks against them.

CBIS 15: Networking

Prepares students for careers in network engineering by exploring the design, analysis, and support of local area networks (LANs) and complex data communication environments.

Innovation at BCC

FAA-Certified Drone Program

At BCC, we are taking education to the skies. Based on our feasibility studies, we are building an exciting drone program that explores the intersection of autonomous flight technology, supply chain automation, and 3D mapping.

This initiative helps support local operations like the Barstow International Gateway (BIG). Students receive hands-on training to prepare for FAA Part 107 Commercial Drone Pilot credentials.

The Campus Makerspace

Our Makerspace is a cornerstone of creative, hands-on learning. It serves as an incubator for students to turn coding, GIS, and engineering concepts into physical reality. The space is equipped with:

  • 3D Printers for rapid prototyping
  • Cricut & Silhouette Vinyl Cutters
  • Soldering stations and electronics toolkits

Robot Navigation: Atan vs Atan2 Explorer

I designed an interactive scrollytelling experience to explain the critical mathematical concepts behind robotic pathfinding and game design.

Standard trigonometry (atan) often fails when navigating a full 360-degree plane due to canceled negative signs, causing robots to drive the wrong way. Atan2(y, x) safely resolves true angles across all four quadrants.
